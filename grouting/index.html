Saskatoon’s subsurface is a textbook of glacial history, and anyone who’s excavated near the South Saskatchewan River knows the transition from dense till to loose alluvial sands can happen within meters. This variability creates real challenges for foundation support and groundwater control, particularly beneath the silty sand lenses common in the Sutherland and Nutana areas. A properly engineered grouting design program anticipates these transitions rather than reacting to them during construction. For projects near the riverbank where artesian conditions occasionally appear, we often combine grouting strategies with in-situ permeability testing to calibrate injection pressures and mix designs before full-scale work begins.

Effective grouting in Saskatoon is less about the grout itself and more about reading the glacial stratigraphy — miss a thin sand lens and you miss the flow path.

Technical details of the service in Saskatoon

CSA A23.3 and the National Building Code of Canada set the baseline, but grouting design in Saskatoon must also account for the high plasticity of the glacial till that caps much of the city. This till can heave under excessive injection pressure while simultaneously masking deeper voids in the underlying Tyner Valley sediments. Our approach uses staged permeation grouting for medium-to-fine sands — microfine cements and colloidal silica are specified when the effective grain size drops below 0.2 mm — and compaction grouting where till density needs improvement beneath existing footings. A CPT test profile across the site provides the continuous stratigraphy needed to define injection horizons: grout takes in the sand units can be an order of magnitude higher than in the interbedded clay seams, and the CPT’s sleeve friction data helps pinpoint those transitions without the disturbance caused by conventional sampling.

Typical technical challenges in Saskatoon

A trailer-mounted colloidal mixer and high-pressure piston pump are standard on Saskatoon jobsites, paired with digital flow meters and pressure transducers that log data every 5 seconds. The real risk in this city comes from uncontrolled grout travel into utility corridors — Saskatoon Light & Power duct banks and the aging combined sewers in older neighborhoods like Riversdale can intercept grout if injection is not confined by sleeve-pipe or packer isolation. We establish refusal criteria based on volume limits per stage, not just pressure, because an open-graded backfill around a sewer trench can accept grout indefinitely without building back-pressure. Thermal monitoring on exothermic cement grouts is also mandatory when injecting near shallow geothermal lines, a growing consideration as more buildings in the city adopt ground-source heat systems.

Our services

Our Saskatoon grouting work spans new construction and remedial applications across the city’s distinct geotechnical zones:

Permeation and Curtain Grouting

Designed for the sandy alluvial deposits flanking the river, using microfine cements and colloidal silica to create low-permeability barriers that cut off groundwater flow into excavations and basements.

Compaction and Compensation Grouting

Which grout type works best in Saskatoon’s silty sand conditions?

The answer depends on the grain size distribution obtained from a sieve analysis. For sands with more than 12% passing the #200 sieve, microfine cements with a D95 below 12 microns are typically specified; for cleaner sands, Type III Portland cement with a water-cement ratio between 0.6 and 1.0 by weight can be effective. Colloidal silica is reserved for the tightest formations where even microfine cement cannot penetrate.

What verification testing confirms that the grouting program worked?

We verify grouting effectiveness through a combination of continuous core drilling with photographic logging of grout-filled fractures, falling-head or constant-head packer tests in boreholes to measure post-grouting permeability, and in some cases cross-hole seismic tomography to image the grout bulb continuity between injection points.
