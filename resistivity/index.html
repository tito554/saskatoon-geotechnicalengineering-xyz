CSA C22.3 No. 7 requires accurate soil resistivity data for grounding grid design, and in Saskatoon that means dealing with the complex layers of glacial Lake Agassiz deposits. The city sits at 52.1°N latitude, where winter frost penetrates deep into clay tills and summer saturation shifts resistivity profiles seasonally. We run vertical electrical sounding (VES) surveys using Schlumberger arrays with AB/2 spacings from 1.5 m to 120 m, mapping the transition from unsaturated silts over the Battleford Formation into the stiff Floral till. For projects near the South Saskatchewan River, where sand layers create high-resistivity lenses, we often combine VES with seismic refraction to resolve velocity-resistivity crossovers that neither method alone can untangle.

A resistivity contrast of just 15 ohm-m between the Upper and Lower tills in Saskatoon often marks the boundary where dewatering changes from manageable to high-flow.

Technical details of the service in Saskatoon

The field setup uses a Syscal Pro resistivity meter with 48 or 72 electrodes on a multicore cable, injecting square-wave DC current up to 500 V. In Saskatoon’s residential areas like Nutana or Montgomery, space constraints force us to use Wenner arrays with shorter spreads, which still deliver 15 to 25 m of investigation depth. The instrument compensates for the high contact resistance common in dry surface silts during August, automatically stacking readings until the coefficient of variation drops below 2%. Data processing runs through RES2DINV with a damped least-squares inversion, and we calibrate the inverted resistivity sections against borehole logs from our SPT drilling program to tie ohm-m values to actual grain-size distributions. The resulting 2D profiles show resistivity contrasts as subtle as 5 ohm-m between the Upper and Lower till units, which matters for predicting excavation stability and dewatering rates in deep basements.

Typical technical challenges in Saskatoon

The most common mistake we see in Saskatoon is designers using a single resistivity value for the entire site when the stratigraphy changes laterally within 20 m. Glacial lake plains are not uniform: a thin sand interbed at 6 m depth can drop resistivity from 30 ohm-m to 18 ohm-m, completely altering the cathodic protection requirements for buried steel. One contractor we worked with in the Stonebridge development ignored a low-resistivity pocket mapped by our VES survey, installed standard zinc anodes, and had to excavate the gas line within two years due to accelerated corrosion. The cost of that re-excavation was ten times the survey price. For grounding grid design in substations, IEEE 80 explicitly requires multi-layer soil models derived from VES data, and SaskPower will reject any design that uses assumed values instead of measured resistivity profiles. In the silty clays east of Circle Drive, seasonal moisture cycling creates a surface layer with resistivity 40% lower in May than in September. Our surveys account for that by running two seasonal measurements and providing wet-dry envelope models.

Frequently asked questions

How deep can a VES survey investigate in Saskatoon's glacial soils?

With a Schlumberger array and a maximum AB/2 spacing of 120 m, we reliably reach 70 to 80 m depth in the clay tills south of the river. The practical limit depends on site dimensions, because you need 3 to 5 times the target depth in clear linear space. In tight urban lots we cap investigation at about 25 m using Wenner arrays.

What does an electrical resistivity survey cost for a typical Saskatoon building site?

For a standard VES survey with 3 to 5 soundings across a residential or small commercial lot, budgets range from CA$910 to CA$1,440 depending on array type, number of expansion points, and whether we need to clear vegetation or work around buried utilities.

How do you calibrate resistivity data to actual soil types?

We tie every survey to at least one calibration point, preferably a borehole or test pit with logged stratigraphy. The resistivity values are then correlated to grain size and moisture content using the site-specific relationship, rather than applying generic ranges from the literature, which can be off by 50% in Saskatoon's mixed tills.

Does frozen ground affect the resistivity readings?

Significantly. Ice is a poor conductor, so frozen surface silts can read above 200 ohm-m while the same material at 10°C reads 25 ohm-m. We run surveys between May and October whenever possible. If a winter survey is unavoidable, we use deeper electrode burial below the frost line and apply temperature correction factors per ASTM G57.

What deliverables do you provide after the survey?

The standard package includes raw apparent resistivity data in digital format, inverted 2D resistivity sections with RMS error statistics, a multi-layer soil model for grounding design, and a technical memo summarizing interpreted stratigraphy and corrosivity classification. We can also provide native RES2DINV files if your engineer wants to run alternative inversions.
